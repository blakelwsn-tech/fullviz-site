The problem

Prime Now promised delivery in an hour or two, from small warehouses inside cities. I trained at and helped launch the fourth one in the US, an early Midwest site. Nobody had written down how a building like that should run. Then I launched the seventh, my home site, in Orange County, California.

What I did

We wrote the procedures as we went: receive, stow, pick, pack, ship. I built a labor plan that mixed scheduled staff with flex shifts, and the first staffing model for Amazon Flex drivers at Prime Now. I worked out delivery routes by hand until engineering turned the logic into a product.

When other sites struggled, I was sent to steady them, including one in the UK.

What changed

The network went from 4 warehouses to 24 while I was there, and I helped launch eight of them. My home site in Orange County delivered more than 90% of orders on time, with the largest delivery radius in the country. Three of my four assistant managers were promoted.
